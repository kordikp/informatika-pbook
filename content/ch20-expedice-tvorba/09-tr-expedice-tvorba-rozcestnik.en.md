Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [Piskel](https://www.piskelapp.com)

An editor for pixel art and animated sprites right in the browser; it exports GIFs and PNG spritesheets.

:::reveal How to start
The fastest way from an idea to an animated pixel mascot, with no installation and no AI.

*Free, open source · For 10+, you can draw without an account · in English (the controls are mostly icons)*

1. Open piskelapp.com and click Create Sprite.
2. Set the size to 32 × 32 pixels.
3. Draw the first frame, add a second one and move it a little.
4. Export → GIF and watch your character move.
:::

#### [Lospec: palettes and Pixel Editor](https://lospec.com/palette-list)

A database of about 4,500 pixel art palettes (each downloadable in six formats), a simple editor in the browser and hundreds of tutorials.

:::reveal How to start
A limited palette is the best school of visual language: with 4–8 colours you learn to leave things out.

*Free · Browsing without an account for everyone; the community Discord only from 13 · in English*

1. On lospec.com/palette-list, filter for palettes with 8 colours.
2. Pick one and download it.
3. Load it into Piskel or the Lospec Pixel Editor and draw only with those colours.
:::

#### [BeepBox](https://beepbox.co/)

A chiptune music composer in the browser: a grid of notes, drums, synthesisers, export to WAV and MIDI.

:::reveal How to start
Music from your own notes without AI. The track is saved in the link, so you don’t need an account and nothing is stored on a server.

*Free, open source (MIT licence) · No account, suitable from 10 · in English*

1. Open beepbox.co.
2. Click into the grid to write a 4-bar melody.
3. Add a bass line in the second channel and a rhythm in the drum channel.
4. Copy the page address; that is your saved track. Export → WAV.
:::

#### [Chrome Music Lab: Song Maker](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Song-Maker/)

The simplest way to compose a melody by clicking into a colourful grid (the link to the tool is in a Google article).

:::reveal How to start
For the youngest and for quick sketches: no sign-in, you share by link.

*Free · No account, for everyone · controls with pictures, labels in English*

1. Open Song Maker from the link in the article.
2. Click a melody into the grid and drums at the bottom.
3. Save and download your track as a file.
:::

#### [p5.js Web Editor](https://editor.p5js.org)

An online editor for creative coding in JavaScript: pictures, animations and interactive drawings from a few lines.

:::reveal How to start
Generative art where you write the rules. A large part of the Genuary challenges is built on it.

*Free, open source (Processing Foundation) · You can draw without an account; an account for saving under 13 only with a parent, otherwise download your code or copy it into a file · in English*

1. Open the editor and click Play: you will see an empty canvas.
2. In draw() write circle(mouseX, mouseY, 20) and draw with the mouse.
3. Continue with the workshop in the expedition “A jingle for film night”.
:::

#### [Tinkercad](https://www.tinkercad.com)

3D modelling from blocks and shapes in the browser, by Autodesk, with export for 3D printing.

:::reveal How to start
The easiest first step into 3D: a pixel mascot can be pulled out into blocks and printed.

*Free · Under 13 only with a parent’s consent, or in a class (Classroom) set up by a teacher. Classroom uses a safe mode (Safe Mode) by default · in English, the interface also offers other languages*

1. Ask your teacher for a class code, or create an account with a parent.
2. Create a new 3D design and build the mascot from blocks.
3. Export → STL and ask at the library or a fablab about printing.
:::

#### [Blender](https://www.blender.org)

A professional free program for 3D modelling, animation, rendering and editing. Even feature films are made in it.

:::reveal How to start
There is no ceiling here: the same tool as at Blender Studio, whose films and production files you can take apart.

*Free, open source · No account, installed on a computer; better from about 12 · in English, the interface has translations into other languages*

1. Install Blender (it needs a more powerful computer with a graphics card).
2. On the starting cube, try moving with G, rotating with R and scaling with S.
3. Add a light and render your first picture with the F12 key.
4. Write down how long the render took, and think back to the telling “Why generating takes time”.
:::

#### [Suno](https://suno.com/legal/terms)

An AI generator that makes songs from a text description.

:::reveal How to start
Good for a “human versus machine” comparison and for thinking about who the author is. For music from your own notes, use BeepBox.

*Free version and paid plans (check current prices on the website); a cheaper alternative without AI: BeepBox, free · Not allowed under 13; ages 13–17 only with a parent’s explicit prior consent (terms valid from September 2026) · in English, song lyrics also in Czech*

1. Read the terms with a parent first.
2. Enter a description of the song and generate two versions.
3. Compare them with your own melody from BeepBox (the expedition side trip “Music: human versus machine”).
:::

### 👥 Communities

#### [Genuary](https://genuary.art)

*online generative art challenge (every January) · Free · in English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** No age limit on the website; sharing on social media is usually from 13

1. On genuary.art/prompts, read the 31 January themes. The themes work outside January too.
2. Choose three and make a sketch for them in p5.js, with the turtle or by hand.
3. Share in the class gallery, or with a parent through their account with the hashtag #genuary2026.
:::

> **Stay safe:** You can create offline and without an account. Under 13, don’t share on social media from your own account, only through a parent. Don’t put your name, school or face in the work or in the caption.

#### [Lospec Dailies](https://lospec.com/dailies)

*daily themes for pixel art · Free · in English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** You can read the themes without an account; the Lospec Discord is from 13

1. Open Lospec Dailies and pick today’s theme.
2. Draw it in Piskel with one palette from Lospec.
3. Show your work in the class gallery or at your club. Discord only from 13 and with a parent’s knowledge.
:::

> **Stay safe:** No Discord under 13; reading the website is enough. Don’t open private messages from strangers and don’t move anywhere else with them. If someone wants a photo or contact details, tell an adult.

#### [Tinkercad Classroom](https://www.tinkercad.com/teachers)

*a moderated class for 3D modelling, set up by a teacher · Free · in English, the interface also in other languages*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** The intended route for kids under 13; the teacher arranges parents’ consent

1. Ask your teacher or club leader to set up a Tinkercad Classroom.
2. Join with the class code, not with your own e-mail.
3. Share models within the class, where safe mode is switched on.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Work only inside the class; outside it, an account can publish publicly. Name your model with a nickname, not your name. Sign up for public challenges only with a parent.

### 🏆 Competitions and events

#### [CoderDojo clubs](https://coderdojo.com/)

Volunteer clubs where a child picks their own project (Scratch, web, games) and a mentor helps them. Great for independent, curious makers.

:::reveal How to join
*club · Free (a basic rule of the CoderDojo movement) · Czech*

**When:** Regular meetings in each club; check the current list of clubs and dates on the website (the source is from 2019) **Age:** 7–17 years

1. Find your nearest club (library, company, school) on the CoderDojo ČR website.
2. Book a place at a meeting; younger children come with a parent.
3. Bring a laptop if you have one.
:::

#### [Scratch (MIT)](https://scratch.mit.edu/)

A moderated community where kids share games and animations and get feedback. It follows on from the Hungarian Kovács Mihály/Logo competition.

:::reveal How to join
*creative community · free · dozens of languages including Hungarian*

**When:** all year **Age:** recommended 8–16 years, no fixed limit

1. an account on scratch.mit.edu (younger kids with a parent’s consent)
2. a class account from a teacher
:::

#### [Code Club (Raspberry Pi Foundation)](https://codeclub.org/)

Step-by-step projects (Scratch, Python, web, physical devices) run by volunteers.

:::reveal How to join
*network of clubs · free · projects in many languages*

**When:** during the school year, depending on the club **Age:** about 9–13 years

1. find a club on codeclub.org or start a club at your school
:::

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[Sprite Fright (Blender Studio)](https://studio.blender.org/projects/sprite-fright/pages/about/)**: A short animated film made in Blender. A selection of its production files is freely available under the CC BY licence. *You will learn:* How an animated film is made from storyboard to render, how many frames and how much machine time it costs, and how to credit an open work correctly.
- **[Remixing Blender Studio’s open films](https://studio.blender.org/remixing/)**: The rules for how you may use shots and files from the open films in your own work. *You will learn:* The CC BY licence in practice and the correct line for the credits: “(CC) Blender Foundation | studio.blender.org”.
- **[Piskel on GitHub](https://github.com/piskelapp/piskel)**: The open source code of the pixel art editor that runs in the browser. *You will learn:* How a drawing program works inside: a grid of pixels, layers, animation frames and export to GIF.
- **[Genuary 2026: a selection of demos on CodePen](https://alvaromontoro.com/10-cool-codepen-demos/2026/01/)**: A selection of generative demos from January 2026, among them animated cubes in p5.js for the Genuary challenge. *You will learn:* How a simple rule, chance and repetition become a picture. You can open the code, change it and watch what happens.

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[The Coding Train: p5.js Web Editor](https://thecodingtrain.com/tracks/code-programming-with-p5-js/code/1-intro/2-web-editor)**: Videos in which Daniel Shiffman enthusiastically explains creative coding in p5.js from the very beginning. *(in English (subtitles))*
- **[Lospec: Where to Start](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-where-to-start)**: A signpost for pixel art beginners, plus a library of about 500 tutorials sorted by topic. *(in English)*
- **[Genuary: 31 themes](https://genuary.art/prompts)**: A list of themes for generative art, for example one for each day. An ideal stock of ideas for the whole year. *(in English)*
- **[Lospec: pixel art tutorials](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials)**: Tutorials on shading, animation, palettes and perspective in pixel art. *(in English)*
- **[Suno terms (for parents)](https://suno.com/legal/terms)**: Who may use AI music and who owns the outputs. A good starting point for a conversation at home about age limits. *(in English)*
