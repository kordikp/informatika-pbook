**🔍 Analysis** · step 3 of 5 · [A jingle for film night](#expedice-tvorba-en)

Open [the Sprite Fright film page](https://studio.blender.org/projects/sprite-fright/pages/about/) and play the film. Then take it apart like a filmmaker.

**1. Production line.** Put the stages of making an animated film in order: editing, storyboard (a drawn script), character models, idea, render, animatic (a storyboard in time with sound), animation, lighting, script, sound. Then find on the film’s pages which of these stages have published files.

**2. Numbers.** The film is roughly 10 minutes long, at 24 frames per second. How many frames is that? Let’s say one frame takes 30 minutes to render on one computer (real times vary by scene). How long would one computer take to render the film? How many computers do you need to finish it in a week?

**3. Visual language.** Find three things the film simplifies or exaggerates: character shapes, colours, details. Why exactly those?

**4. Licence.** Write a line in the credits for a shot from the film.

**Check:** 10 × 60 × 24 = 14,400 frames. 14,400 × 0.5 h = 7,200 h, that is 300 days on one computer. A week has 168 hours, so you need about 43 computers. Credit: “Sprite Fright, (CC) Blender Foundation | studio.blender.org, CC BY”.
