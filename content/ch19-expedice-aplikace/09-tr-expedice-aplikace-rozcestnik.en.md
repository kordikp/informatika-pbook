Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [MIT App Inventor](https://appinventor.mit.edu)

Mobile apps for Android put together from blocks in the browser, tested on your own phone via the AI Companion app and a QR code.

:::reveal How to start
It follows on from Scratch and the book already knows it. The Global Appathon is built on it, so there’s a straight road from the workshop to the competition.

*Free · No age limit in the tool; you sign in with a Google account: under 15 with a school account or an account managed by a parent · Interface in several languages; guides and forum mainly in English*

1. Open appinventor.mit.edu and click Create Apps.
2. Sign in with a school account or together with a parent.
3. Start a project and drag in a TextBox, a Button and a ListView.
4. Connect your phone via Connect → AI Companion and scan the QR code.
:::

#### [Code.org App Lab](https://code.org/applab)

Apps in the browser: you design screens in Design Mode, save data in Data Mode, and the blocks switch to real JavaScript.

:::reveal How to start
The smoothest bridge from blocks to text code: you see the same program in both forms.

*Free · Meant for roughly 13 and up; younger users through a class set up by a teacher · In English*

1. Work through the intro course Intro to App Lab.
2. In Design Mode, draw a screen with a button and a field.
3. Switch the blocks to text and read the JavaScript that appears.
4. Save expenses in Data Mode and check that they survive a page refresh.
:::

#### [Thunkable](https://thunkable.com/)

Apps for Android and iPhone by dragging elements and blocks; a nicer default look than App Inventor.

:::reveal How to start
For when you want your app on iPhone too and care more about design.

*A limited free plan (sources disagree); publishing from paid plans of roughly 19–59 USD a month, discounts for students. Cheaper alternative: App Inventor · The terms don’t give a clear age: use it only with a parent or through school · In English*

1. Go through the price list with a parent and choose the free plan.
2. Start from a template and replace the content with your own screens.
3. Try the app in the browser preview; don’t pay for anything.
:::

#### [Penpot](https://penpot.app)

Screen design and clickable prototypes in the browser, with open code (MPL 2.0 licence).

:::reveal How to start
You draw your app’s screens and test them with people before you write the first block. Free without a school licence.

*Free for individuals · The terms on the home page don’t state an age: set up the account with a parent · In English*

1. Set up an account with a parent (Sign up, it’s free).
2. Create a file and three phone-sized boards.
3. Draw three screens of your app.
4. Link the buttons with arrows in prototype mode and let a friend try it.
:::

#### [Figma for Education](https://www.figma.com/education/)

The most widely used tool among interface designers, with a free education plan.

:::reveal How to start
Most professional teams work in it, so it’s good to know. For younger users, Penpot is better.

*Free through a verified school (at least 10 students, renewed every year) · On your own from 13 (in the EU only from 16 in some countries). Younger users only through a school that signs the school terms · In English*

1. Ask your computing teacher whether the school has Figma for Education.
2. Open a blank file in the school team.
3. Draw your app’s screens and link them into a prototype.
:::

### 👥 Communities

#### [Global Appathon (MIT App Inventor)](https://www.appinventor.org/appathons)

*online appathon · Free · In English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** Open to people from all over the world; age categories are announced each year; younger people with a parent or teacher

1. With a parent or teacher, keep an eye on the Appathons page; registration is usually in February.
2. Choose a topic from that year’s theme (in 2026, the UN Sustainable Development Goals).
3. Build an app in App Inventor and submit it by the deadline (in 2026, 15 April).
:::

> **Stay safe:** Register with an adult, don’t show real names or photos of children in the app or the presentation, and don’t pay for anything: the competition is free.

#### [Technovation Girls](https://technovationchallenge.org/program-overview/)

*year-long competition with a mentor · Free to take part · In English, in some places with local clubs*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** Girls aged 8–18, age groups 8–12, 13–15 and 16–18

1. With a parent or teacher, find a team (1 to 5 people) and an adult mentor.
2. Register the team; the registration deadline is usually in March.
3. Over the season, build an app for a problem in your area, plus a short business plan.
:::

> **Stay safe:** The team is led by an adult mentor; communicate through the competition’s platform, not private messages, and don’t share personal data in app demos.

#### [Swift Student Challenge (Apple)](https://developer.apple.com/swift-student-challenge/eligibility)

*yearly competition · Free (free developer registration with Apple) · In English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** In Czechia from age 15, in Slovakia and Hungary from 16; you must be a student

1. You need an iPad or a Mac with the Swift Playgrounds app.
2. Build a small app in the Swift language (in 2026, Swift Playground 4.6 or Xcode 26).
3. Submit it by the deadline, which is usually at the end of February.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Register for the developer account with a parent, never give anyone your Apple account password and don’t reply to messages promising a “guaranteed win”.

#### [App Inventor Community](https://community.appinventor.mit.edu)

*forum · Free · In English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** Anyone can read without an account; posting with an account from 13, younger people through a parent or teacher

1. Read without an account: search for the name of a block or an error message.
2. If you want to ask something, write the question with a parent and attach a picture of the blocks, not the whole project with its data.
3. Reply with what helped, so the next person finds out too.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Don’t publish your name, school or contact details, don’t share a project with your friends’ real data, and don’t move to private chats with strangers.

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[Spliit](https://github.com/spliit-app/spliit)**: An open-code app for splitting shared expenses (MIT licence), about 3,000 stars. It splits a bill equally, by shares, by percentages and by exact amounts, suggests how to settle up and works as a web app on your phone’s home screen. *You will learn:* How a real app solves exactly the task from the core “Who owes whom how much”: it saves individual expenses and works out the balances from them. You’ll also see the split into frontend (Next.js, React) and backend with a PostgreSQL database.
- **[Winning apps of the Global Appathon 2026](https://www.appinventor.org/news/app-inventor-for-social-good-powered-by-aws)**: App Inventor apps by students from 141 countries for the Sustainable Development Goals: emergency services, crop health, sustainability. *You will learn:* How people your age describe a real problem in one sentence and build an app with a single main feature.
- **[Penpot as an open project](https://penpot.app)**: A big open-code design tool that runs entirely in the browser. *You will learn:* What a design system looks like: components, variants and colour tokens. A prototype made with it also shows the finished CSS and SVG, so you can see the bridge between design and code.

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)**: 30 illustrated principles of interface design: Fitts’s law, Hick’s law, Miller’s law, the Von Restorff effect and more. *(In English)*
- **[Intro to App Lab](https://studio.code.org/s/applab-intro)**: A short Code.org course: your first app in App Lab, blocks and JavaScript. *(In English)*
- **[App Lab documentation](https://curriculum.code.org/docs/applab/)**: A description of all App Lab blocks and commands with examples; handy when you move to text. *(In English)*
- **[Thunkable documentation](https://docs.thunkable.com/)**: Guides to components, data and publishing in Thunkable. *(In English)*
- **[The book Laws of UX (2nd edition)](https://oreilly.com/library/view/laws-of-ux/9781098146955)**: Jon Yablonski’s book on the principles from the website, explaining the psychology behind them. Paid, for advanced readers; the website is free. *(In English)*
