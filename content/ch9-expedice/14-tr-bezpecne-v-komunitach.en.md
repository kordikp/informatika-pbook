Competitions, forums and clubs are a great place to find people with the same hobby. Here is how to join them without giving away more about yourself than you want to.

On the internet you meet people you do not know. Most of them are happy to give advice and help. But a few rules protect you, your accounts and your data.

### Ten rules

1. **Set up accounts with a parent.** Up to a certain age the law requires a parent’s consent (the table is below). Never change your age when you register.
2. **A nickname instead of your name.** Do not put your real name, school, town, phone number or a photo of your face in your profile, code or comments. Watch out for the signature in your commits and for data stored in photos, too.
3. **Passwords and keys belong only to you.** Never send them in a chat and never upload them to a public repository. Where you can, turn on two-step verification.
4. **Start with moderated communities.** School clubs, competitions, seminars and forums with rules and moderators are the safest start.
5. **Talk in public.** Ask in the public channel, not in private messages from adults you do not know. If someone pushes you into a private conversation, tell a parent or the organiser.
6. **Only meet up with someone you trust.** Go to training camps, summer camps and meet-ups only with an organisation your parents know. Never meet a person from the internet without a parent.
7. **Gifts and secrets are a warning.** Anyone who offers you money, gifts, a job or a “secret project”, or wants you to keep something from everyone, is acting suspiciously.
8. **Do not run other people’s programs.** A file or script from a stranger can steal your account. Practise security only where it is allowed, for example in CyLab Security Academy (formerly picoCTF). Testing other people’s websites without permission is illegal.
9. **Write to the point and politely.** Ask about code and tasks, not about personal things. If you do not like the tone of a discussion, you can leave it at any time.
10. **Watch your time and sleep.** A competition should be fun, not stress. It is OK to skip a round.

### From what age your own consent is enough

A service that processes your data based on consent needs a parent’s consent up to a certain age. This age is called the **age of digital consent** and it is different in every country:

| Country | Age of digital consent |
|---|---|
| Czechia | [15 years](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2019-110) |
| Slovakia | [16 years](https://www.slov-lex.sk/ezbierky/pravne-predpisy/SK/ZZ/2018/18/) |
| Hungary | [16 years](https://www.whitecase.com/publications/article/gdpr-guide-national-implementation-hungary) |

On top of that, every service has its own age limit in its terms. The most common ones:

- **GitHub:** 13, or the country’s higher age of digital consent. *Parental consent:* Not under 13. The digital consent clause may mean 16 in Hungary (check how it is interpreted). ([terms](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service))
- **Discord:** 13, or a higher local limit. *Parental consent:* Not under the limit. ([terms](https://discord.com/terms))
- **Scratch:** no fixed limit (recommended 8–16). *Parental consent:* Yes, a parent is responsible for a younger child. ([terms](https://scratch.mit.edu/))
- **Kaggle:** under 16 only with a parent’s consent. *Parental consent:* Yes, a parent fills in the consent form. ([terms](https://www.kaggle.com/learn))
- **Hugging Face:** 13. *Parental consent:* Not under 13. ([terms](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/esafety-guide/hugging-face))
- **itch.io:** 13 (users). *Parental consent:* You can publish content from 18, or earlier with a parent’s consent. ([terms](https://itch.io/docs/legal/terms))
- **Reddit:** 13, or a higher local limit. *Parental consent:* Between 13 and adulthood a legal guardian must approve the terms. ([terms](https://redditinc.com/policies/user-agreement))
- **YouTube:** 13. *Parental consent:* Younger kids only via YouTube Kids or a supervised account that a parent allows. ([terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms))
- **Replit:** 13. *Parental consent:* Under 18 you need a parent’s permission. ([terms](https://replit.com/terms-of-service))
- **Roblox:** no fixed limit, default restrictions apply under 13. *Parental consent:* Yes, parental controls are available remotely. Under 13, messaging outside games is limited, and in-game messaging needs a parent’s consent. ([terms](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7386393))
- **CyLab Security Academy (picoCTF):** 13. *Parental consent:* 13–17 with a parent’s e-mail, or an account from a teacher. ([terms](https://cylabacademy.org/terms.html))
- **Khan Academy:** no limit, under 13 via a parent. *Parental consent:* Yes, a parent approves or creates a child account (an unapproved account is deleted). ([terms](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202487460-If-my-child-is-under-age-13-what-login-options-are-there-))
- **edX (CS50):** 13. *Parental consent:* Not under 13, there is no route with a parent’s consent. ([terms](https://support.edx.org/hc/en-us/articles/13481901497239-Can-anyone-use-edX))
- **OpenStreetMap:** 13. *Parental consent:* Not under 13. ([terms](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/53579/are-there-any-age-limit-restrictions-for-osm-contributors))
- **Stack Overflow:** 13. *Parental consent:* Not under 13. ([terms](https://stackoverflow.com/legal/terms-of-service/public))

Limits change. Before you set up an account, read the service’s current terms with a parent.

### When something goes wrong

When someone writes you nasty things, threatens you or wants something from you:

1. Do not reply and do not send anything.
2. Take a screenshot and do not delete the message. It is evidence.
3. Block the sender and report them to the service’s moderators.
4. Tell an adult you trust straight away.

In Europe, the **116 111 helpline** helps children and young people: in Czechia Linka bezpečí, in Slovakia Linka detskej istoty, in Hungary Kék Vonal. The call is free and you do not have to give your name.
