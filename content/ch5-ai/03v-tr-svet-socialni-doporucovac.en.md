Three times you stop at a video with a puppy. The next day your feed is full of puppies. You never told anyone you like them. The app worked it out by itself.

The feed is chosen by a **recommender**, an algorithm that learns from your **signals**. The strongest one isn’t a like, but **watch time**: which video you stopped at, and for how many seconds. Then **skipping**, when you scroll away after a second, replaying, sharing, commenting and following the creator. And on top of that, the behaviour of **similar users**: people who watched the same things as you also watched this, so it offers it to you too.

![A feed over three days: from a varied mix to a bubble](images/svet-socialni-feed.en.svg)

A **feedback loop** forms. You watched puppies, so you get more puppies, so you watch puppies, so you get even more. The feed narrows, and you don’t even know what it didn’t offer you. That is a **filter bubble**. With puppies it is sweet. With news, politics or videos about how you should look, not any more: the bubble shows you a one-sided world, and it seems as if everyone thinks that way.

Why does the network do it? It is free, and **advertisers** pay for it. It sells your **attention**: the longer you watch, the more ads you see. That is why there is **infinite scroll**, why there is **autoplay**, why there are notifications. This is called **addictive design**.

But you can influence your feed, because it is built on your signals. Tap *Not interested* on what you don’t want, skip straight away, search for something new, follow creators outside your bubble. And if you want to see how well the feed knows you, look at a friend’s feed for a while.

**Try it!** Open your feed and write down the topic of ten videos in a row. How many topics repeat? Then for three days tap Not interested on one of them and watch what changes.
