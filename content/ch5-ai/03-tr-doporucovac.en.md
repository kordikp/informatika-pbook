You watch one cat video. Five minutes later your feed is full of cats. Who decided that?

A social network has millions of videos. Only a few fit into your feed, and they are picked by a **recommender system**. It watches **signals**: what you click on, **how long you watch**, what you skip, what you share and what people with similar taste watch. Then it offers you more of what will most likely keep you there.

Imagine that for a few rounds you click mostly on cat videos. Next time the feed shows you more of them. Because you see more of them, you click on them even more. Try it in the mini-game: can you fight your way out of a one-topic bubble?

After a few rounds the feed is full of one kind of video. The algorithm learns from your clicks, and your clicks are shaped by what it showed you. This **feedback loop** creates a **bubble**.

Why does the network do this? It is “free” because it earns money from advertising. **The longer you watch, the more ads you see.**

**Try it!** For a week, give your feed deliberate signals about one new topic (space, say): a few searches, videos watched to the end, likes. Watch how long it takes for the topic to appear by itself. You have just seen your profile at work.

> **Watch out, a common mistake:** “The feed shows the best or the newest.” The feed shows whatever will most likely keep you there longest. And it doesn't know you only by your likes. It also counts how long you look at what.
