You upload a video you worked on all weekend, and 200 people see it. Your friend’s video about nothing gets seen by 200,000. Who decided that?

The **recommender**, the platform’s algorithm. It first shows your video to a small group of people and watches the **signals**: how many of them watched to the end (**watch time**), how many **skipped** it after two seconds, who liked it, shared it, commented, played it again. When the signals look good, it shows the video to a bigger group, and then a bigger one still. Most of all to people who behave like those who liked it (**similar users**).

![How the recommender decides on a video’s reach](images/svet-tvorba-dosah.en.svg)

That is why creators work so hard on the first two seconds, and why they stretch videos so that you watch to the end. The platform doesn’t reward what is best, but what **holds attention**. Attention is what it sells to **advertisers**: the longer you watch, the more ads you see. This is called the **attention business model**. And with it comes **addictive design**: **infinite scroll**, **autoplay** of the next video, notifications saying “someone liked your post”.

A **feedback loop** forms. Whatever got reach gets filmed more, because it works. Creators copy successful formats, viewers see them even more, and the feed narrows. For viewers this becomes a **filter bubble**. For creators it is a trap: they film what the algorithm wants, not what they want themselves.

That doesn’t mean a good video has no chance. You just know what the decision is based on. A video that people watch to the end and pass on spreads by itself. A video made only “for the algorithm” gives them no reason to.

**Try it!** Pick two videos by the same creator: one with a big reach and one with a small one. What is different in the first three seconds? And who might watch the video right to the end?
