“Create a quiz app about space: ten questions, points and a result.” A minute later you have it, and it looks finished. But is it really finished?

This way of working is called **vibecoding**: you describe what you want, AI writes the code, you try it out and refine it. It is a huge thing. An idea that used to need years of learning, you can have in an afternoon. But AI gave you a **draft**, not the truth. For the app you send to your classmates, **you are responsible**.

![Comic: An app in five minutes](images/comic-iluze.en.svg)

What that means in practice:

- **Check it, don’t trust the preview.** Tap twice quickly, fill in nothing, enter nonsense. Does the app count the points correctly? Check the answers in the quiz in a **second, independent source**: AI will confidently write that Mars has three moons. It has two.
- **Ask who claims it, how they know and why.** When AI writes “this code is secure”, that is predicted text, not a check.
- **Read what it gave you, at least roughly.** Where are the answers saved? Does the app send something out?

**When not to use AI:** when you are learning something and AI would do it for you, such as an assignment that is meant to teach you loops. When you would put **sensitive data**, passwords or your classmates’ details into the prompt. And when the app is supposed to **make decisions about people**: who gets a worse duty, or who isn’t allowed on the trip.

And **say** where AI helped you: *the code was written by AI from my design; I tested it and fixed the scoring*. It is not a confession of guilt. It is information people need so they know what to trust.

**Try it!** Have AI write a small app or page for you, such as a click counter. Try to break it in three ways and write down what you had to fix. Finally, add an honest sentence about what the AI did and what you did.
