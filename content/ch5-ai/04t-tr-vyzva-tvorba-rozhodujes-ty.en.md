For the open day video you have five great pieces from the internet. But at the end it shows the logo of the local bakery that paid for the video. And suddenly it is not about what looks nice, but about what you are allowed to use.

<!-- task -->

The video will run on the school YouTube channel and the bakery paid for its logo. So this is **commercial use**. You have these pieces:

1. An opening shot from an open film by Blender Studio, licence CC BY.
2. Background music, CC BY-SA.
3. A second track, CC BY-NC.
4. An illustration of the school, CC BY-ND. You want to draw the logo into it.
5. A mascot from an AI generator, made from one prompt of yours.

**Tasks:**
- a) For each piece, decide: allowed, allowed with a condition, or not allowed.
- b) What licence must the whole video have, and why?
- c) Write a line in the credits for pieces 1 and 2 in the form “title, author, source, licence”.
- d) A classmate downloads the mascot and prints it on a T-shirt. Can you forbid it?

**Success criterion:** all four parts match the solution and you justify each decision with one part of the licence.

<!-- hint -->

CC licences are made of parts: BY = credit the author, SA = share alike, NC = non-commercial only, ND = no changes. CC0 = no conditions.

<!-- hint -->

Under Creative Commons rules, music laid under a picture counts as an adaptation of the work. What does that mean for the SA part?

<!-- hint -->

Copyright in Czechia and across the EU protects a work created by a human. How much human creative work is there in the output of one prompt?

<!-- solution -->

**a)** 1: allowed, credit the author. 2: allowed, but the whole video must have the same licence. 3: **not allowed**, NC forbids commercial use and the sponsor makes the video commercial. 4: **not allowed**, ND forbids changes and an added logo is a change. Without changes the illustration would be fine. 5: allowed, if the generator’s terms permit it and the mascot does not copy someone else’s character.

**b)** **CC BY-SA.** The SA track is combined with the picture, so the result must get the same licence.

**c)** For example “Sprite Fright, (CC) Blender Foundation, studio.blender.org, CC BY 4.0” and “Track title, author’s name, link to source, CC BY-SA 4.0”.

**d)** Probably not. Under the prevailing interpretation, output from one prompt without further creative work by a human is not a copyrighted work, so it does not belong to you. And the whole video has the BY-SA licence, so others may use it if they credit the source. If you redraw and finish the mascot by hand, your share grows.

<!-- more -->

On Blender Studio’s page about remixing, read the rules and put together a 20-second remix from their open films with the correct credits. Then play the extra game “Can you put it in a sponsored video?”.
