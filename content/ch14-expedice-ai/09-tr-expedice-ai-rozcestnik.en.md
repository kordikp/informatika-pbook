Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [Teachable Machine](https://teachablemachine.withgoogle.com/)

A web tool from Google where you train a model on images, sounds or poses from a camera or microphone. No programming.

:::reveal How to start
The fastest way from “AI learns from examples” to your own model. You see straight away how the make-up of the data changes the mistakes.

*free · Without an account from age 11, with a parent or teacher knowing. Saving to Google Drive needs a Google account, and that only with a parent’s permission · interface in English*

1. Open the page and choose Get Started, then Image Project.
2. Name 2–3 classes and take at least 40 photos of each in different light.
3. Train Model, then test it in the Preview window on new shots.
:::

#### [micro:bit CreateAI](https://createai.microbit.org/)

You collect movements with the micro:bit’s accelerometer, train a gesture model and then program it in MakeCode.

:::reveal How to start
Machine learning on your own body: waving, clapping, jumping. It fits the world of robots and a club too.

*free, you need a micro:bit V2 (schools or clubs often have one) · from age 7 (according to microbit.org), no sign-in · English, the website also in French, Polish and others, Czech is not listed*

1. On microbit.org/createai click Try CreateAI.
2. Connect the micro:bit and record at least 3 classes of movements, each 10× and a little differently each time.
3. Train the model, test it and open it in MakeCode.
:::

#### [Machine Learning for Kids](https://machinelearningforkids.co.uk/)

You train a model on text, images or numbers and add it to Scratch as a block. The website comes with about twenty projects with instructions.

:::reveal How to start
A bridge between AI and programming: the model becomes a block in your game. The interface is also in Czech.

*free · from age 12 (according to the author’s book). An adult creates the class account; the samples can be tried without one too · interface in Czech and English, project worksheets in English*

1. In the language menu, choose Čeština.
2. Choose the try-it-out mode or sign in through your class.
3. Start with a text recognition project and use the model in Scratch.
:::

#### [TensorFlow Playground](https://playground.tensorflow.org/)

A live neural network in the browser. You change layers, neurons, learning rate and noise, and watch how the network learns to separate points.

:::reveal How to start
Your first look inside a neural network. You’ll see why one straight line isn’t enough for a spiral or XOR.

*free, open source · no account, for sharp kids from age 12 · English*

1. Choose the data with four squares (XOR) and remove all hidden layers.
2. Start training with the play button and watch the test loss.
3. Add one layer with 2 neurons and compare.
:::

#### [Transformer Explainer](https://github.com/poloclub/transformer-explainer)

An interactive visualisation of the GPT-2 language model right in the browser. You type the start of a sentence and see the probabilities of the next token.

:::reveal How to start
A follow-on from the trip “Inside a chatbot”: a real model, not made-up numbers.

*free, MIT licence · no account, from age 13 (harder and in English) · English*

1. On GitHub, open the link to the live demo in the project description.
2. Type the short start of an English sentence.
3. Find where the model shows the probabilities of the next token, and compare them with your own guess.
:::

### 👥 Communities

#### [Teens in AI: Global AI Techathon](https://www.teensinai.com/)

*online competition (techathon) and workshops · Techathon free (according to competition listings), some of the organisation’s courses are paid · English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** ages 13–18

1. Fill in the application with a parent, who confirms the contact details.
2. Look for a team at school or in a club, not among strangers on the internet.
3. Under 13: for now just read the tasks and the results of past years, with a parent or teacher.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Online video calls with mentors only with a parent’s consent. Don’t share your address or phone number, and don’t post photos in the team chat without consent.

#### [International Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence (IOAI)](https://mbzuai.ac.ae/news/international-olympiad-in-ai-launches-to-nurture-the-next-generation-of-ai-talent)

*international olympiad in AI · taking part through a national team · English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** secondary school students, upper limit around age 20; the 2026 edition is in Abu Dhabi

1. For ages 11–15 this is a goal for later: you can already read the sample tasks now.
2. Find out through your computing teacher whether the Czech Republic or Slovakia has a national selection.
3. Practise on tasks with data and models, not just theory.
:::

> **Stay safe:** No registration on your behalf without a parent and the school. Watch out for paid “preparation courses” that promise you a place.

#### [Day of AI (MIT RAISE)](https://dayofai.org/)

*a school programme and a day of AI activities · free · English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** all of primary and secondary school, with a separate level for lower secondary

1. Show your teacher that the materials are free; the teacher registers.
2. Suggest one lesson in your class, for example about machine creativity.
3. Share the results in the book’s class gallery, not on a public website.
:::

> **Stay safe:** The whole class takes part through the teacher; you don’t create any account. Photos of classmates only with their consent.

### 🏆 Competitions and events

#### [Hour of Code / Hour of AI (CSEd Week)](https://www.csedweek.org/)

The new Hour of AI version is an easy way into AI and programming. Gifted kids can try the more advanced tutorials here.

:::reveal How to join
*campaign and online activities · free · many languages including Hungarian, Czech and Slovak*

**When:** CS Education Week 2026: 7–13 December 2026 (sources differ by a day, check on code.org) **Age:** from age 4 up

1. you can do the one-hour activity at home or with your class on code.org
:::

#### [CS50x (Harvard, edX)](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/)

A university introduction to computer science, from C through Python and SQL to the web. For motivated 14- to 15-year-olds it is a big challenge.

:::reveal How to join
*online course · the course is free, a verified certificate costs money · English (subtitles in many languages)*

**When:** The new CS50x 2026 version runs from 1 January 2026, you can start the course at any time **Age:** edX from 13; the content suits 14+ better

1. materials free on cs50.harvard.edu
2. assignments via edX or GitHub (mind the account’s age limit)
:::

#### [Kaggle Learn and competitions](https://www.kaggle.com/learn)

Short practical courses in Python, pandas and machine learning on real data, in a notebook in your browser.

:::reveal How to join
*courses and competitions in data and AI · free · English, Python*

**When:** all year **Age:** under 16 only with a parent’s consent via a form

1. a parent sets up the account and fills in the consent
2. tip: do not post in discussions until you are 18
:::

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[Quick, Draw! and its dataset](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/gcp/drawings-in-the-cloud-introducing-the-quick-draw-dataset)**: A drawing game in which a neural network guesses your doodles. The games produced over 50 million drawings in 345 categories, published under the CC BY 4.0 licence. *You will learn:* How a huge dataset is created by volunteers, what is missing from it or biased, and why the licence decides who may use the data.
- **[Transformer Explainer (source code)](https://github.com/poloclub/transformer-explainer)**: The open code of the visualisation that runs GPT-2 in the browser. *You will learn:* How text turns into tokens and numbers, and how the model builds the probabilities of the next word from the numbers.
- **[TensorFlow Playground (the authors’ article)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03788v1)**: An article about how and why Playground was made. It says it is written in JavaScript without the TensorFlow library. *You will learn:* That a neural network is just calculation that fits into a web page. Inspiration for your own visualisation.
- **[ML-Machine → micro:bit CreateAI](https://cs.au.dk/news-events/news/show-news/artikel/microbit-createai-unleashes-ai-creativity-in-classrooms)**: A tool from Aarhus University that started in Danish schools and became CreateAI. *You will learn:* How a research tool for schools turns into a product for millions of children, and what it has to be able to do to work in a classroom.

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[Elements of AI](https://elementsofai.com)**: An online course on the basics of AI from the University of Helsinki, brought into Czech by prg.ai with ČVUT and UK. Theory with exercises, at your own pace and with a certificate. *(Czech, Slovak, English and others)*
- **[Elements of AI: new topics and Czech video guides](https://prg.ai/en/popular-ai-basics-course-expanded/)**: Course news: current topics and, in the Czech version, teaching videos. Good for older and persistent learners. *(English (page), course in Czech)*
- **[The book Machine Learning for Kids (Dale Lane)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/645860/machine-learning-for-kids-by-dale-lane/)**: A book of projects from age 12: sorting pictures of animals, rock-paper-scissors against the computer and more, all in Scratch. *(English)*
- **[Developing AI literacy with the micro:bit](https://microbit.org/teach/lessons/developing-ai-literacy-with-the-microbit/)**: A series of lessons with CreateAI and MakeCode for ages 11–14. A good curriculum for a club. *(English)*
- **[AI4K12: TensorFlow Playground](https://ai4k12.org/tensorflow-playground)**: A catalogue of tools for teaching AI, here with a description of Playground. From there you’ll also find Quick, Draw! and other experiments. *(English)*
