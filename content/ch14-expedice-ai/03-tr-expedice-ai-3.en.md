**🔍 Analysis** · step 3 of 5 · [The bird feeder model](#expedice-ai-en)

**Quick, Draw!** is a game by Google: you have 20 seconds for a picture, and a neural network guesses what you’re drawing. The games produced a **dataset** (a collection of data) with more than 50 million drawings in 345 categories. Google published it under the **CC BY 4.0** licence, so anyone may use it as long as they credit the author ([about the dataset](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/gcp/drawings-in-the-cloud-introducing-the-quick-draw-dataset), in English; [about the game on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick,_Draw!)).

Analyse it like a journalist:
1. **Who created the data?** The players. Did they get anything for it? Did they know they were drawing for training?
2. **What is missing from the data?** The drawings were made in a hurry. What does a “typical” drawing of a house probably look like? And will the model recognise a house that looks different from what most people imagine?
3. **Where might the model fail?** Think of three categories where people from different countries draw things differently.
4. **Licence:** what may you do with the data? What do you have to credit?

If you play the game, remember: your drawing may become part of public data. It’s anonymous, but don’t draw anything personal in it.
