**👥 Crew** · step 4 of 5 · [Internet at the cabin](#expedice-stroje-a-site-en)

The **European Astro Pi Challenge** is run by the European Space Agency together with the Raspberry Pi Foundation. It’s free and open to everyone up to 19. The **Mission Zero** category is for beginners: in an online editor you write a short program in Python, and it shows your picture on the colour display of the Astro Pi computer on the ISS. The average participant is 12, and in the 2025/26 round over 24,000 kids took part. The instructions are in English, and the code is short.

You already know the route your program takes from the chapter Machines and networks: from the browser to a server, from the server to mission control, and from there by radio to the station flying 400 km above you.

**How to join:**

1. Find a mentor: a computing teacher, a club leader or a parent. They submit the entry for the team.
2. On astro-pi.org, read the rules for the current round and open the Mission Zero editor.
3. Write and test your program in the emulator, and send it in with your mentor.

The second option is **Coolest Projects**: an online showcase of projects by makers up to 18, free. You can even enter your network from Filius with a short video.

**Three rules for taking part safely:**

1. An adult handles entries and communication with the organisers. You don’t send any personal details.
2. Don’t put your full name, address, school or other people’s faces into the code, picture or video without their consent.
3. Communicate only through the competition’s official website. If someone contacts you elsewhere or something makes you uncomfortable, tell your mentor.

> **Stay safe:** before you join a crew, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en). Set up your account with a parent and never give your address, school or phone number.
