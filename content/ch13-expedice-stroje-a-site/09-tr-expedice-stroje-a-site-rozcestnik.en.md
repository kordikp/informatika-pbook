Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [Filius II (and Filius Classic)](https://filius-ii.net)

A computer network simulator for schools: you connect computers, switches, routers, DNS and a web server with cables and watch where the packets go. The best bridge from the `sit` demo to a real network. You build your own internet without any risk of breaking something.

*free, open source (GPL v3) · from 11 (school software, no account) · interface in German or English; Czech translation not verified*

**How to start:**

1. Open Filius II in your browser (it’s in beta), or download Filius Classic from https://www.lernsoftware-filius.de for Windows, Mac and Linux.
2. Place two computers and a switch, connect them with cables and give them the addresses 192.168.0.10 and 192.168.0.11.
3. Switch to simulation, install the command line on the first computer and type ping 192.168.0.11.

#### [Cisco Packet Tracer](https://www.netacad.com/cisco-packet-tracer)

A professional network simulator from Cisco. You set up its routers with the same commands as in a real company. For when Filius is no longer enough. Future network engineers learn on Packet Tracer, so it really raises the ceiling.

*free, but only with a Cisco Networking Academy account · for older and advanced users (13+); the account age limit is not verified, so create the account with a parent or through a teacher · in English*

**How to start:**

1. With a parent or teacher, create an account on netacad.com and sign up for the intro course “Getting Started with Cisco Packet Tracer”.
2. Download Packet Tracer only from the official page, because other websites offer modified versions.
3. Rebuild the network from Filius: two computers, a switch, ping.

#### [v86: Linux in the browser](https://copy.sh/v86/)

An emulator of a whole computer running in a browser tab. You can boot Linux, old DOS or other systems in it. You get to handle an operating system from the inside: processes, memory, files. And you can’t break anything at home; just close the tab.

*free, open source · from 11, no account · in English (Linux commands are English abbreviations)*

**How to start:**

1. Open copy.sh/v86 and pick a small Linux (for example Alpine or Buildroot).
2. Wait for the command line to start, then type ls, then free and ps.
3. Create a file with the command echo hello > test.txt and close the tab. Is the file gone after you start it again? Why?

#### [NandGame](https://nandgame.com/)

A puzzle in the browser: starting from a single NAND gate, you build an adder, memory, a processor and finally a whole computer, step by step. It answers the question the kitchen metaphor leaves open: how a processor actually calculates.

*free · from 12, no account · in English*

**How to start:**

1. Open nandgame.com and go through the first levels with gates.
2. Once you’ve built a half adder, compare it with the extra game “How the cook calculates”.
3. Goal for the first week: build an adder for four-bit numbers.

#### [Turing Complete](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1444480/discussions/0/3081016748994069893)

A game on Steam in which you go from a NAND gate all the way to your own processor and assembly language (assembler). For those who got hooked on NandGame. It’s longer, with a story and with challenges to build the smallest circuit.

*paid game (check the price on Steam); you can try something similar for free in NandGame · Steam requires an account from 13; younger players play through a parent’s account · in English*

**How to start:**

1. Agree with a parent whether they’ll buy the game on their account.
2. Go through the first chapter with gates.
3. Compare your adder with the solution in NandGame. Which one has fewer gates?

### 👥 Communities

#### [European Astro Pi Challenge: Mission Zero](https://astro-pi.org/)

*competition by ESA and the Raspberry Pi Foundation · free · in English (you program in Python); check the website for other language versions*

**Age:** up to 19; the average Mission Zero participant is 12

**How to join:**

1. Agree with a teacher, club leader or parent who will be your mentor.
2. Write a short program in Python in the Mission Zero online editor.
3. If it passes the checks, your program will run on a computer on the ISS and you’ll get a certificate.

> **Stay safe:** An adult mentor handles the entry. Don’t write your full name, address or school in the program or the message. Communicate only through the competition’s official website.

#### [Coolest Projects (online showcase)](https://online.coolestprojects.org/about-us)

*international online showcase of projects by young makers · free · in English*

**Age:** up to 18

**How to join:**

1. Pick a finished project, for example your network from Filius or your Linux commands.
2. Film a short video with no faces and no personal details.
3. Send the entry with a parent or teacher (in 2026 the deadline was 27 May).

> **Stay safe:** Don’t show other people’s faces in the video without their consent. Don’t give your address or school. Leave the entry and communication with the organisers to an adult.

#### [Code Club](https://blog.codeclub.org/author/georgiafowlerf5d463d921/)

*network of after-school coding clubs by the Raspberry Pi Foundation · free · in English; local clubs depending on the country*

**Age:** 9–13

**How to join:**

1. Ask at school or at the library whether a Code Club or a similar club runs nearby.
2. If not, show the page to your computing teacher. An adult can start a club.
3. At the club, suggest a project from the expedition, for example Mission Zero.

> **Stay safe:** The club is run by a checked adult. Create online accounts only with a parent. Don’t share personal details online, not even with other members.

### 🏆 Competitions and events

#### [CyLab Security Academy, formerly picoCTF (Carnegie Mellon)](https://cylabacademy.org/)

*cybersecurity competition and practice website · free · English*

Ethical hacking in a safe environment. It teaches Linux, cryptography and web security as a game.

**When:** The big competition is held once a year, the challenge library (formerly picoGym) is open all year. Check the 2027 date on the website **Age:** from 13; for ages 13–17 a parent’s e-mail, or a teacher sets up the account

**How to join:**

1. register on cylabacademy.org with a parent’s e-mail
2. or a class account from a teacher

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[v86: a computer written in JavaScript](https://copy.sh/v86/)**: An open project that imitates an x86 processor and the hardware around it so faithfully that Linux boots on it. *You will learn:* Hardware can be described by a program. If you can imitate a processor, you really know what a processor does.
- **[Build the Hack computer (Nand2Tetris)](https://github.com/VitorgsRuffo/Building-The-Hack-Computer/blob/main/README.md)**: A student project based on the Nand2Tetris course: from gates through a processor to a simple computer in the Logisim simulator. *You will learn:* How memory and a processor are put together from gates. The code and the method are public, so you can go step by step (in English).
- **[Young people’s code on the ISS (Astro Pi)](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/an-astronomical-anniversary-young-peoples-code-heads-to-the-international-space-station/)**: Programs by kids and teenagers that run on Raspberry Pi computers on the International Space Station. *You will learn:* Even a small computer with sensors is a full machine with an operating system. And the data from space travels to Europe over a network.

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[How DNS Works (comic)](https://howdns.works/)**: A comic about a resolver that looks for an IP address via the root servers and the domain servers. It has been translated into many languages. *(in English and other languages (Czech not verified))*
- **[How does DNS work? (Julia Evans)](https://drawings.jvns.ca/dns)**: A single hand-drawn page that explains all of DNS in one picture. *(in English)*
- **[Life of a DNS query (Wizard Zines)](https://wizardzines.com/comics/life-of-a-dns-query/)**: A comic about the journey of one DNS query, step by step and with caching. *(in English)*
- **[Skriptum Netzwerk Filius 2026](https://www.lernsoftware-filius.de/downloads/Skriptum_Netzwerk_Filius_2026.pdf)**: A textbook with exercises for Filius, from your first network to DNS and the web. Handy as a store of further exercises. *(in German)*
