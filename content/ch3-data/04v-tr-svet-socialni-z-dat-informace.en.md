After a week your channel has numbers: views, followers, watch time. Lots of numbers, not much clarity. How do you get something useful out of them?

Start with a **question**. Data says nothing by itself. You get information out of it only when you ask: *what time is best to post? which video do people watch to the end? is the channel growing, or standing still?* That is the first step of the **data cycle**: question, data, chart, interpretation.

**You choose the chart by the question.** Is the channel growing? That is **change over time**, a line chart of followers day by day. Which video was the best? A **comparison**, bars side by side. Where did the viewers come from: the feed, search or sharing? A **proportion**, a pie or a stacked bar.

![Followers over a week: a misleading axis and an honest axis](images/svet-socialni-statistiky.en.svg)

Creator apps love drawing charts that look like a rocket. Look at the **axis**: when it starts at 950 followers instead of zero, growth from 960 to 990 looks like a steep hill. In reality it is three per cent.

And be careful with conclusions. Videos posted on Friday evening get more views. Is that because it is Friday? Or did you post that funny cat video on Friday, which would have worked any time? Two things that go together (**correlation**) don’t have to mean that one causes the other (**causation**). To know, you would have to post similar videos on different days.

Finally, ask **where the data comes from and what is missing from it**. The statistics show how many people started the video, but not how many of them only had it playing in the background. And they don’t show what people thought of it.

**Try it!** Ask one question about how you use social media, for example: on which day do I watch the most? What data would you need, what chart would you draw, and what is missing from the data?
