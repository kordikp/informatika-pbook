You put a photo in your story. Does your group of friends see it, or the whole school? And what about that unknown account that has been following you since last week?

A social network is an **information system**. It keeps records of your posts, followers and messages, and it lets people in different **roles** do different things. On your own profile you are the **administrator**: you decide on the **access rights**. Who may see a post (**reader**), who may comment (**commenter**), who may tag you or message you. Even a group chat has roles: the **group admin** adds people and changes the name; a member only writes.

![Who sees a post: from “only me” to “public”](images/svet-socialni-kruhy.en.svg)

The **principle of least privilege** applies: everyone gets only as much as they need. A photo from a party belongs to close friends, not to all your followers. Comments from strangers can be switched off. And the class group doesn’t need everyone to be an admin; two people who look after it are enough.

Watch out for the **link for everyone**. An album set to *anyone with the link* can be forwarded to anyone. And someone can film a close-friends story with a second phone. Privacy settings protect only inside the system. Once something has left it, no access right can stop it any more.

And then there is **purpose**. Every system keeps data for some need. Your need is to share with friends. The network’s need is to show you ads and to have as much public content as possible. That is why the default settings tend to be more open than you would like, and you have to switch privacy on yourself.

**Try it!** Go through the privacy settings on a network you use. Who sees your posts, who may message you and who may tag you? Is that how you want it?
