Your parents set up your phone so games only work until eight in the evening. You can't switch it off, they can. Who decides this?

An **information system** keeps data for a **purpose**: a school system keeps marks and absence notes, an online shop keeps orders. People in it have different **roles**, and each role has different **rights**. Some may only **read**, others may **change** things, and the administrator decides on everyone else's rights.

In the school system everyone sees something different. **Jan** sees his own marks and timetable, but not his classmates' marks. **Jan's mum** sees Jan's marks and writes absence notes. **The form teacher** enters marks, because that's her job. **The head teacher** sees everything and decides on the timetable and the roles, but doesn't change marks. The same data, four different screens. And when you share a document, you are the administrator: who gets which right?

The system knows who you are, and according to your role it shows you only what you need. This is called the **principle of least privilege**: everyone gets only as many rights as they need for their work.

In the school system it looks like this:

| Who | What they see | What they can change |
|---|---|---|
| **Jan**, pupil | his own marks and timetable | nothing |
| **Jan's mum** | Jan's marks | absence notes |
| **the form teacher** | the whole class's marks | marks |
| **the head teacher** | everything | the timetable and roles, but not marks |

When you share a document, you are the administrator. An **anyone with the link** link can reach anyone at all. So share with named people and give only the rights that are needed.

**Try it!** Take a school system you use and find out what you can see in it and what your parent or teacher can see. What can only you change? What can only they see? And why is it probably like that?

> **Watch out, a common mistake:** “What I can see, everyone can see.” An information system shows each role something different. A pupil sees their own marks, a parent their child's marks, a teacher the whole class.
