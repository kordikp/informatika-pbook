Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [CyberChef](https://gchq.github.io/CyberChef/)

A “Swiss army knife” for encodings, ciphers and hashes from the British GCHQ. It runs entirely in the browser; operations are put together into a recipe by dragging with the mouse.

:::reveal How to start
The standard tool of CTF players. It’s handy both for cracking layers (hex, Base64, ROT13) and for building your own tasks. No account and no installation.

*free, open source · no limit (needs no account) · in English*

1. Open the page, find the From Hex operation on the left and drag it into the recipe.
2. Paste text into the input and watch the output. Add ROT13 and change the shift (Amount).
3. Try the Magic operation, which guesses the encoding by itself.
4. Never paste your real passwords into the tool; only try practice ones.
:::

#### [CyLab Security Academy challenge library (formerly picoGym)](https://cylabacademy.org/learners.html)

A year-round library of practice tasks from past years of picoCTF, the biggest online CTF for school students, from Carnegie Mellon University, now CyLab Security Academy.

:::reveal How to start
The tasks are graded from complete basics to hard and sorted into categories (cryptography, forensics, web). You can train any time, not just during the competition.

*free · account from age 13, under 18 with a parent’s consent; younger kids play with a parent through the parent’s account or with a club · in English*

1. Set up an account together with a parent (a nickname without your name and year).
2. In the challenge library, filter for the easiest tasks in the General Skills category.
3. For each task, write down which principle was in it.
:::

#### [OverTheWire: Bandit](https://overthewire.org/wargames/bandit/)

A “wargame” for complete beginners. On each level you connect to a server through the terminal (SSH) and look for the password to the next level.

:::reveal How to start
It teaches you to control Linux and the terminal, which is the basis of all further security work. The servers are built to be “attacked”, so it’s legal.

*free (voluntary donation) · no age given; younger kids are advised to do it with an adult (terminal work) · in English*

1. On the page, open Level 0.
2. On Windows open PowerShell, on a Mac or in Linux open Terminal, and connect with the ssh command following the instructions.
3. Write down every password you get in your notebook, not on any website.
:::

#### [CryptoHack](https://cryptohack.org/faq/)

A platform for cryptography in the form of tasks: from encodings and XOR to RSA and elliptic curves.

:::reveal How to start
For those who enjoy the maths side of security most. Tasks are solved with short Python programs, so security and programming come together.

*free (voluntary donations via Patreon) · no age given; account and Discord only with a parent · in English*

1. Go through the Introduction to CryptoHack course.
2. Install Python 3 and the PyCryptodome library (following the FAQ).
3. Solve the first tasks on paper and only then write them as code.
:::

#### [TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/about)

Online cybersecurity courses with virtual computers in the browser: defence, attack, forensics.

:::reveal How to start
The most thorough path from the basics to a career. Suits older and advanced learners who have already done the CyLab challenge library or Bandit.

*the basic part is free, premium content is paid (cheaper alternative: the CyLab challenge library and OverTheWire for free) · from age 13 (terms of use, version 2.0 of 28 August 2026); younger kids only read along with a parent · in English*

1. Set up the account with a parent, without your real name.
2. Start with the free introductory rooms.
3. Don’t take a paid subscription without agreeing it with your parents.
:::

#### [Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/terms/)

An open-source password manager: a vault that remembers long, unique passwords for you, on your computer and on your phone.

:::reveal How to start
It directly meets the goal of the core “Account and password” (password manager). The free version has no limit on the number of passwords or devices.

*free; Premium about 10 USD a year · from age 13 according to the terms (the privacy policy says 16 or the local minimum); in the Czech Republic, so set it up with a parent, ideally as part of a family vault · in English (website and terms)*

1. With a parent, choose one long master password made of 5 random words.
2. Turn on two-step verification for the vault.
3. Gradually change your service passwords to random ones generated by the vault.
:::

### 👥 Communities

#### [CyLab Security Academy: the big competition (formerly picoCTF)](https://cylabacademy.org/competitors.html)

*CTF competition for school students (online, teams of up to 5 people) · free · in English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** from age 13; prizes only for students at schools in the USA

1. Form a team with classmates or with a club.
2. Under-13s play only through an adult’s account (a teacher’s or a parent’s) and under their supervision.
3. Between competitions, train in the CyLab challenge library.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Under 18, an account needs a parent’s consent; no personal details in the team chat; open task files only in CyberChef or in the browser, don’t run them.

#### [CryptoHack Discord](https://cryptohack.org/faq/)

*online community (Discord) for cryptography tasks · free · in English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** CryptoHack gives no age, Discord is from age 13; younger kids only read along with a parent

1. First solve the introductory course without the chat.
2. Join the Discord only with a parent and the parent’s own account, or just read.
3. Ask about principles, not ready-made solutions.
:::

> **Stay safe:** No private messages with strangers; don’t share your name, school or photos; anyone offering solutions for a file or a password is a scammer.

#### [OverTheWire chat](https://overthewire.org/information/chat.html)

*community chat for wargame players · free · in English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** no age given; for children only with an adult

1. First go through a few Bandit levels on your own.
2. Open the chat with an adult and just read how others ask questions.
3. Never write level passwords in the chat.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Don’t share level passwords or personal details; don’t click links from strangers; don’t run anything someone sends you.

### 🏆 Competitions and events

#### [CyLab Security Academy, formerly picoCTF (Carnegie Mellon)](https://cylabacademy.org/)

Ethical hacking in a safe environment. It teaches Linux, cryptography and web security as a game.

:::reveal How to join
*cybersecurity competition and practice website · free · English*

**When:** The big competition is held once a year, the challenge library (formerly picoGym) is open all year. Check the 2027 date on the website **Age:** from 13; for ages 13–17 a parent’s e-mail, or a teacher sets up the account

1. register on cylabacademy.org with a parent’s e-mail
2. or a class account from a teacher
:::

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[Have I Been Pwned: Pwned Passwords and k-anonymity](https://www.troyhunt.com/understanding-have-i-been-pwneds-use-of-sha-1-and-k-anonymity/)**: An article by the service’s author about how you can check whether a password has leaked without the server seeing it: only the first 5 characters of the SHA-1 hash are sent. *You will learn:* Hashes, k-anonymity and designing a service that protects privacy by the way it’s built. In English, but you can manage it with a translator.
- **[CyberChef: source code and releases](https://github.com/gchq/CyberChef/releases)**: The open code of the CyberChef tool on GitHub, including versions to download and run offline. *You will learn:* How a big tool is put together from small operations. Why an intelligence agency publishes its code: openness builds trust.
- **[CyLab Security Academy: competitions](https://cylabacademy.org/competitors.html)**: The rules and organisation of the biggest online CTF for school students: teams of up to 5 people, task categories, prizes. *You will learn:* How a fair competition is built: rules, categories, teams. Useful as a model for your own mini-CTF in the expedition.

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[Introduction to CryptoHack](https://cryptohack.org/courses/intro/)**: A step-by-step introductory cryptography course: encodings, XOR, first scripts for connecting to tasks. *(in English)*
- **[freeCodeCamp: Bandit step by step](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/improve-you-cybersecurity-command-line-skills-bandit-overthewire-game-walkthrough/)**: A detailed guide to the first levels of OverTheWire Bandit with explanations of the commands. It contains the solutions, so only open it after your own attempt. *(in English)*
- **[How to Use CyberChef: The Beginner's Guide (2026)](https://hackerdna.com/blog/how-to-use-cyberchef)**: An up-to-date guide to CyberChef: recipes, operations, running offline. *(in English)*
