**🔍 Analysis** · step 3 of 5 · [The case of the late bus](#expedice-data-en)

**Part 1: Spurious Correlations.** Open [tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations](https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations) (in English). The author compared a huge number of data series and picked pairs that are “linked”, for example the number of master’s degrees in education and the popularity of one meme. Choose **two** pairs and for each one write:
- How many years (points) does the graph have? Is that a lot or a little?
- Do the axes start at zero? Do both axes have the same scale?
- Is it chance, or could there be a third thing that affects both?

The website itself explains why it works: if you dig through thousands of pairs, some will look alike purely by chance. Work it out: if one pair in a thousand came out “beautifully” by chance and the author compared 25,000 of them, how many beautiful coincidences would he find? (25.)

**Part 2: Our World in Data.** Open [ourworldindata.org](https://ourworldindata.org/) and find a graph on a topic that interests you. Find for it:
1. where the data come from (the source);
2. under what licence you may use the graph (usually CC BY: you may, if you credit the author);
3. what exactly is measured (the definition) and for which years;
4. one thing the graph **doesn’t show**.

**Result of the analysis:** a table “honest graph vs. trick” with six points, which you’ll use as a checklist for your poster.
