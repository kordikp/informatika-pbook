Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [CODAP (Common Online Data Analysis Platform)](https://codap.concord.org/)

A web app for exploring data: you drag in a CSV, and by dragging you turn the table into a graph, groups, a mean and a median. The new version is v3.

:::reveal How to start
It is made for school pupils. It does what a spreadsheet does, but makes you think about the data, not about formulas. It builds on the cores “Table: data in order” and “From data to information”.

*free, open source · from year 5 (according to the authors for grades 5–14) · English; the community translates it into other languages, Czech not checked*

1. Open codap.concord.org and start the app. According to the Concord Consortium, you need no account and no installation.
2. Drag a CSV file into the window or open a sample document.
3. Drag a column name onto a graph axis and watch how the points group together.
:::

#### [Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/pricing)

A journalists’ tool for clean graphs, maps and tables from data you paste in. The free plan can publish and download PNGs.

:::reveal How to start
It teaches honest graphs: axis, labels, data source. Good for a final poster or a website.

*free (with the line “Created with Datawrapper”); teachers can ask for a free education space · no age given on the pricing page; you can create a graph without registering, saving to an account only with a parent or through a teacher’s account · English, German (Czech version of the interface not checked)*

1. On the website, click to create a graph without registering.
2. Paste in data from a table (copy and paste) and choose the type of graph.
3. Add the data source and axis labels, then download a PNG.
:::

#### [Gapminder Tools](https://www.gapminder.org/teaching/materials/)

Hans Rosling’s bubble chart and other tools (Bubbles, Maps, Ranks, Trends, Dollar Street) using data about every country in the world.

:::reveal How to start
It shows how to fit four values and time into one graph. There is also an offline version you can load your own data into.

*free · no limit, no account · English and other languages*

1. Open the bubble chart and play the animation from the year 1800.
2. Change what is on the x and y axes and watch how the story changes.
3. Find Czechia and compare it with two of its neighbours.
:::

#### [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/)

A non-profit website with thousands of interactive graphs about health, climate, energy and education.

:::reveal How to start
Every graph has a source and a licence (usually CC BY), so you learn to ask “where do the data come from” on a real example.

*free, open licence · no limit, no account · English*

1. Find a graph on a topic that interests you (for example energy from renewable sources).
2. Switch the graph to a map or a table.
3. Read the source and the licence under the graph.
:::

#### [SQL Murder Mystery](https://mystery.knightlab.com/)

A detective story in your browser: you can only solve the murder in SQL City with queries to a database in the SQL language.

:::reveal How to start
The most fun first step into databases. It builds on the challenge “Two Emas and one key”.

*free · no account; the content is a murder mystery, better from about 12 · English*

1. Open the game and go through the walkthrough for beginners.
2. Write your first SELECT query on the table crime_scene_report.
3. Join two tables through a shared column.
:::

### 👥 Communities

#### [ISLP: international statistical poster competition 2026–2027](https://iase-web.org/islp-poster-competition-2026-2027)

*competition · free · English (international round), national round in the country’s language*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** category A 9–12 years, B 13–15 years, C 16–18 years

1. A team has 2 to 5 pupils (a poster by one person is not accepted) and chooses its own topic.
2. You enter through the national round, usually through your school or a teacher. Deadlines differ by country, so check them with the national coordinator.
3. The international deadline is 14 March 2027; the winners will be presented at the World Statistics Congress in Lusaka in July 2027.
:::

> **Stay safe:** A teacher or parent handles the entry. Don’t put names, photos or addresses of the people from your survey on the poster. Collect survey data anonymously and with consent.

#### [Zooniverse: citizen science](https://www.zooniverse.org/get-involved)

*community · free · English, some projects in other languages too*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** no minimum age; under 16, a parent or legal guardian must approve the registration

1. Choose a project (animals in camera traps, galaxies, old manuscripts) and try a few tasks, even without an account.
2. Set up an account only together with a parent.
3. On the Talk discussion forum, ask about the data, not about yourself.
:::

> **Stay safe:** An account only with a parent. In the forum, don’t write your real name, school or town. If someone messages you privately about something off topic, don’t reply and tell an adult.

#### [The CODAP community (Concord Consortium)](https://concord.org/newsletter/2015-fall/building-codap-community)

*a community of teachers and makers · free · English*

:::reveal How to join
**Age:** for teachers; a child reads without an account

1. Show your computing or maths teacher that there are ready-made activities with CODAP.
2. Look through the sample documents the community shares.
3. Suggest a club lesson at school working on your class’s own data.
:::

> **Stay safe:** Only read, without an account. Leave sharing your own documents to a teacher. Don’t put classmates’ personal data into the data.

### 🏆 Competitions and events

#### [Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/)

Story-based puzzles you can solve in any language. Great for solving together in class or as a family.

:::reveal How to join
*online challenge · free · English, any programming language*

**When:** from 1 December (the 2025 edition had only 12 days), check the 2026 edition on the website **Age:** no limit, better from about 13 with some programming experience

1. you log in with an account (GitHub/Google), so mind the account’s age limit
2. a private leaderboard with your class or club
:::

#### [CS50x (Harvard, edX)](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/)

A university introduction to computer science, from C through Python and SQL to the web. For motivated 14- to 15-year-olds it is a big challenge.

:::reveal How to join
*online course · the course is free, a verified certificate costs money · English (subtitles in many languages)*

**When:** The new CS50x 2026 version runs from 1 January 2026, you can start the course at any time **Age:** edX from 13; the content suits 14+ better

1. materials free on cs50.harvard.edu
2. assignments via edX or GitHub (mind the account’s age limit)
:::

#### [Kaggle Learn and competitions](https://www.kaggle.com/learn)

Short practical courses in Python, pandas and machine learning on real data, in a notebook in your browser.

:::reveal How to join
*courses and competitions in data and AI · free · English, Python*

**When:** all year **Age:** under 16 only with a parent’s consent via a form

1. a parent sets up the account and fills in the consent
2. tip: do not post in discussions until you are 18
:::

#### [Khan Academy – Computing](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing)

Interactive courses in JavaScript, drawing with code and SQL at your own pace.

:::reveal How to join
*online courses · free · English, some content translated*

**When:** all year **Age:** under 13 via a parent’s or teacher’s account

1. a parent creates a child account, or a teacher creates a class
:::

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[Spurious Correlations (Tyler Vigen)](https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations)**: Thousands of pairs of data that are “beautifully” linked, and yet it is chance. For example the number of master’s degrees in education and the popularity of one meme. *You will learn:* Why digging through lots of data always turns up something similar. Each pair comes with sources and the calculation; the website itself explains the tricks: searching blindly, a cut-off axis, too few points.
- **[Trendalyzer and Gapminder’s bubble chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trendalyzer)**: The software Hans Rosling used to show how the world changes with animated bubbles. Today it lives on as Gapminder Tools. *You will learn:* How to encode four values (x axis, y axis, size, colour) and time into one picture, and when that helps a graph and when it confuses (in English).
- **[Our World in Data: graphs with a source and a licence](https://ourworldindata.org/)**: A non-profit project that collects world data and adds a source and a licence to every graph. *You will learn:* What an honest graph looks like: labelled axes, source, definition, CC BY licence. Take one graph apart and find everything it tells you about where the data come from.
- **[CODAP as an open-source project](https://concord.org/our-work/research-projects/codap/)**: A research project of the Concord Consortium: a data app for schools that any developer can change. *You will learn:* How a tool for teaching data is made, and why it is free and open (in English).

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[SQL Murder Mystery: walkthrough for beginners](https://mystery.knightlab.com/walkthrough.html)**: Step by step from the first table to SELECT and WHERE queries and joining tables. Good to do before the detective story itself. *(English)*
- **[CODAP: getting started guide (PDF)](https://codap-server.concord.org/wp-content/uploads/codap-start-up-guide.pdf)**: A short guide to opening data, making a graph and grouping points. *(English)*
- **[Gapminder: teaching materials](https://www.gapminder.org/teaching/materials/)**: Quizzes and tasks that show how wrong our guesses about the world are, and how to fix them with data. *(English and other languages)*
