You download a torch app. As soon as it starts, it wants access to your contacts, your location and your microphone. It can shine without them. So why?

Because some apps live on data, not on shining. They sell your contacts and location or use them for advertising. So with every app, ask: **does it need that permission for what it does?** Maps need your location, a torch doesn’t. A camera app needs the camera, a calculator doesn’t. You can give a permission only for the time you are using the app, and take it back in the settings at any time.

Worse are apps that pretend to be something else. A **Trojan horse** looks like a game or a “phone cleaner” and quietly reads text messages with codes or shows ads. Most often it comes from a file downloaded outside an **official store**: an “improved” paid app for free, an app from a link in a text message. Official stores check apps, and even so something slips through now and then. Outside them, nobody checks. **Ransomware** locks your files and demands a ransom. A **virus** spreads on by itself. All of it together is called **malware**.

Every app and system has **bugs**, and attackers look for them. The maker fixes a bug with a **patch** and sends it out as an **update**. That is why you update, even when the app “works”: fixing a hole that attackers know about is more important than new features.

When you build your own app, it works the other way round. Ask only for the permissions you really need, and explain why. A user who sees unnecessary requests deletes the app.

And in case something goes wrong: keep your photos and important files **somewhere else too**, in the cloud or on a computer. **A copy elsewhere** survives a lost phone, a broken display and ransomware.

**Try it!** Open the permission manager in your phone’s settings and see which apps have access to your location. Can you find an app that doesn’t need it? Take it away.
