You look at some trainers in an online shop. Then you open the news, a video and a game, and everywhere the same trainers stare at you from an ad. How do those websites know?

With **cookies**. A cookie is a small text file that a website saves in your browser and reads on your next visit. Some are useful: thanks to them you stay logged in and the online shop remembers your basket. It is a **passive trail**: you didn’t write anything, and yet a trail of you remained.

Other cookies don’t belong to the website you are on but to a **third party**. A news site puts in an ad from an ad network, and the network saves its own cookie in your browser. The same network has ads on thousands of websites, so it recognises you everywhere and puts together what interests you: trainers, games, which city you are in. That is what **targeted advertising** is sold from. Data about you has value, and that is why companies collect it, and so do **scammers**.

![The journey of one advertising cookie across three websites](images/svet-weby-cookies.en.svg)

That is why a cookie banner pops up on websites. In the **EU**, under the **GDPR** and the rules on cookies, a website needs your consent for tracking and advertising cookies. Necessary cookies, for example for logging in or for the basket, don’t need consent. Refusing should be as easy as accepting, even though websites like to hide the *Reject* button.

The other half of the trail is **active**: what you write and upload yourself. When you build your own website, don’t put your address, phone number or photos of classmates on it without their consent. What you publish once, you can’t take back. Even if you delete the page, someone may have saved it.

**Try it!** Open your favourite website in a private window and click Settings in the cookie banner. How many partners, that is third parties, are there? How many of them does the website really need in order to work?
