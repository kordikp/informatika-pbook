A photo from your balcony: a sunset, nothing special. But anyone who looks carefully will find out where you live, which school you go to and when you are not at home.

A photo carries two kinds of trail. What you can see in it: the church behind the house, the school logo on your hoodie, the number of the tram, the name on the doorbell. And what you can’t see: **metadata**. Your phone stores the time, the phone model and often the **location** from GPS with the photo. Some networks delete it when you upload. But when you send the photo as a file, the metadata goes with it.

![Everything one photo gives away](images/svet-socialni-fotka.en.svg)

That is your **active footprint**: what you publish yourself. Your **passive footprint** builds up without you writing anything: what you look at, for how long, where you log in from, what **cookies** websites have stored on your device. The two fit together. From ten holiday photos you can piece together where you live and when you are away on holiday.

Who collects this trail? The network and **third parties**, advertising companies, for **advertising**. But also **scammers**: the more they know about you, the more believable the message they can write to you. And people who want to get to know children under a false identity.

What to do about it:

- **Privacy settings:** a private profile, location only while using the app, no automatic location tags.
- **Before you post:** look at the background. What could a stranger read from the photo?
- **Don’t share for other people:** a photo of a classmate belongs to them too. Ask before you put it out or tag them.
- **Your rights:** in the EU, under the **GDPR**, you can ask the network to show you or delete what it has about you. But what someone has once saved or forwarded, you **can’t take back**.

**Try it!** Pick the last photo you posted and look at it through a stranger’s eyes. What could they read from it? Then look at the photo’s info in your phone’s gallery. Is the location there?
