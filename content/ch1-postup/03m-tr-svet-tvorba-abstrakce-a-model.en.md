There is no photo of a person on a toilet door. There is a little figure made of a circle and a few lines. And everyone in the world knows what it means.

An icon is a **model**: it leaves out everything it doesn’t need for its **purpose**. On the door the purpose is a single one: to be recognised in a second, from a distance and in any language. Hair colour, clothes or a face don’t help with that, so they disappear. This is called **abstraction**. What matters depends on the purpose.

![From a photo through a drawing to an icon](images/svet-tvorba-ikona.en.svg)

The **visual language** of a whole picture works the same way. A poster for the school dance has one purpose: to make people stop and find out when and where. That is why it has one main thing the eye lands on (**composition**), a few colours that go together, strong **contrast** between the text and the background, and one readable font. A poster with five fonts and a rainbow of colours left nothing out, and that is why nobody understands it. When everything is important, nothing is.

**No model is complete**, and it is always a **choice**. An icon on a map in your phone leaves out different things from an icon on a poster. A YouTube video thumbnail leaves out almost the whole video: what remains is one face, strong contrast and three words.

A model can also be a **graph**. A plan for a video, a storyboard, is a series of scenes (**nodes**) and the cuts between them (**edges**). In an interactive story the edges branch depending on what the viewer chooses, and the graph shows you straight away a scene that nobody can reach.

And why bother, when AI generates the picture? That is exactly why. AI will churn out ten designs for you in a minute. What to leave out of them, and which one works for your purpose, is your decision.

**Try it!** Draw an icon for your favourite activity so that a classmate recognises it in three seconds. What did you leave out? And what would happen if you left out a little bit more?
