**🔬 Side trip** · [The fact-checking team](#expedice-socialni-en)

Scientists from Cambridge found that people who try out disinformation tricks in a game recognise them better afterwards. Play **Bad News** (about 15 minutes, you play a spreader of lies with six tricks) and **Harmony Square** (10 minutes, you stir up trouble in a peaceful town square). For each trick, write down one example you know from your feed.

Then design **your own vaccine game** for younger kids, for example for your cousin Bára. Use a game type from the book: in a **hotspot** game, the player clicks on the suspicious parts of a fake post. Write 5 posts, each with one or two clickable clues, and a “why” sentence for each one. Watch the criterion: every clue must point to a **trick**, not a topic. “There’s an exclamation mark” is not a trick, “it rushes you so you have no time to think” is.

Share the finished game in the class gallery. Don’t use real posts by classmates or real names.
