You type “my class on a trip to the mountains” into a generator and add a photo. A moment later the picture is ready. But where was it actually made?

Not in your phone. The prompt and the photo travelled over the internet to the **server** of the company that runs the generator. A server is a computer that **provides a service** to others. This one calculates pictures for millions of people at once. It stands in a **data centre**, a hall full of graphics cards, and you use it remotely. That is the **cloud**: **someone else’s servers** that you rent, or get for free.

![The journey of a prompt to the data centre and of the picture back](images/svet-tvorba-cesta.en.svg)

The advantage is clear: a picture your laptop couldn’t manage, you have in a moment and anywhere. There are three **dependencies**. Without a **connection** you generate nothing. The **company** can make the service more expensive, change it or shut it down, and your history disappears with it. And your data is **with the company**: the prompt, the uploaded photo of your classmates and the result. Depending on the terms of the service, it may keep them and sometimes even use them for further training.

The data centre stands in some **country**, and that country’s laws apply there. For companies in the **EU** and for the data of people in the EU, the **GDPR** applies: the company has to say what it does with your data, and delete it if you ask. A photo of a classmate’s face is their personal data. Uploading it without their consent is a bad idea, wherever the server stands.

Some models run **locally**, right on your computer. Then the data goes nowhere and it works even without the internet, but you need powerful hardware and the results tend to be weaker. And what you have only in the cloud, keep **backed up somewhere else**: download the pictures and projects that worked out, don’t rely on the history in the service.

**Try it!** In the terms of a service you use for pictures or for chatting with AI, find the answers to two questions: May it use my inputs for training? And can that be switched off?
