Your marks in the school app look the same on your phone and on a computer. That is because they aren’t on either of them.

Almost every app has two parts. The **frontend** is what you see and control: screens, buttons, lists. It runs on your device. The **backend** works on a **server**, a computer that **provides a service** to thousands of users at once. It keeps the data in a database, checks logins, calculates and enforces the rules. The frontend sends requests, the backend answers. The marks of the whole school are on the server, and that is why you can see them from any device.

An app’s server is usually in the **cloud**, on **someone else’s servers** in some company’s data centre. For you that has **advantages**: you have your data everywhere, you can lose your phone and nothing disappears, your friends see the same as you. And **dependencies**: without a connection the app can’t do much, and when the company closes down, raises its prices or blocks your account, the data is gone.

A quick test: what can your favourite app do on a plane without internet? That is what the frontend does on its own, **locally**. What it can’t do needs the backend. A photo you only have in the cloud won’t open on the plane. A downloaded one will.

So keep important data **in another place too**: photos in the cloud and on a disk at home, a report in the cloud and on your computer. One copy is not a backup.

And where does that server stand? In some **country**, and that country’s laws apply there. For companies in the **EU** and for the data of people from the EU, the **GDPR** applies. The company must tell you what it stores about you and delete it when you ask.

**Try it!** Turn on airplane mode on your phone and go through five apps. What works in them and what doesn’t? That tells you what the frontend does on its own and what needs the backend.
