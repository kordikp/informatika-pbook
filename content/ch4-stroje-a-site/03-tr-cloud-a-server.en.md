Your phone says “Photos backed up to the cloud”. Where are those photos now?

A **server** is a computer that provides a service to others: it sends websites, stores photos, passes on messages. The **cloud** means that your data and programs are on **other people's servers**.

A photo of a dog “in the cloud” travels like this: from the **phone's** storage through the **Wi-Fi router** at home to the internet provider, then along an **optical cable** for hundreds of kilometres, maybe along the sea bed, to a **data centre**, a giant hall full of servers, cooling and backup power. In the end it sits on the **disk of someone else's computer**, perhaps in Ireland or the USA.

The cloud gives you your data everywhere and an extra copy. But you depend on the **connection**, on the **company** and your account with it, and on the **laws of the country** where the server is. In the European Union, personal data is protected by the GDPR.

A **backup** is two copies in two different places. If the only copy is in the cloud, that isn't a backup.

**Try it!** Switch your phone to airplane mode and use it normally for five minutes. Note down what works (the camera, the calculator, downloaded music) and what doesn't (your feed, messages, the AI assistant). You have just split the world into “my device” and “someone's server”.

> **Watch out, a common mistake:** “The cloud is something without a body, up in the sky.” The cloud is computers in data centres, halls full of servers. And data can get lost there too. That's why you back up to two places.
