Your phone is lagging. Will deleting photos help, or closing apps? It depends on which kind of memory is full.

Inside a computer, a phone and a micro:bit, the same parts are at work. Imagine a kitchen:

- **The cook** is the **processor**. It calculates, that is, it cooks.
- **The worktop** is the **main memory** (RAM). On it is whatever is being worked on right now. It is fast and small, and it gets cleared at closing time.
- **The pantry** is **storage**. It is big and remembers permanently, even without power.
- **The shift manager** is the **operating system**. It decides what goes on the worktop and who gets the cook.

Open a game, 40 tabs, your photos and a report. The worktop fills up and the shift manager has to put things away in the pantry and then fetch them back again. That takes time, which is why an overloaded phone lags. You write a few sentences of the report, and before you save them, the power goes off. When it's switched back on, the worktop is empty.

The unsaved report was only on the worktop, in the **main memory**. Without power it forgets. The saved one is in the pantry, in **storage**, and there it lasts.

When a phone says “128 GB”, it means storage. It may have only 8 GB of main memory. A full main memory slows things down, a full storage won't let you save anything new.

The kitchen with its worktop and cook is called **hardware**: things you can touch. The recipes, that is, the programs, are **software**.

**Try it!** In your phone's settings, find how much storage it has and how much of it is free. Then find out (for example on the maker's website) how much main memory it has. Why is storage so much bigger? The cook would tell you: the pantry is always bigger than the worktop.

> **Watch out, a common mistake:** “Memory and storage are the same thing.” When people say “the phone has 128 GB of memory”, they mean storage, the pantry. The main memory, the worktop, is much smaller and is emptied when the device is switched off.
