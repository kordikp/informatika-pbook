You are writing a long report in an app, pop over to a game for a moment, and when you come back the app is empty. The phone closed it. Where did your text go?

It was only in the **working memory** (RAM). That is where apps keep everything that is being worked on right now: the text you are writing, an open photo, the state of a game. Working memory is fast, but **temporary**. When the app closes or the phone’s battery runs out, it forgets. **Storage** remembers permanently. What isn’t saved exists only in working memory.

![Unsaved text in working memory and a saved file in storage](images/svet-aplikace-pamet.en.svg)

Why did the phone close the app? It doesn’t have much memory, say 6 GB, and the game wanted a lot of it. The **operating system** (Android, iOS) decides who gets what. When memory runs low, it closes apps in the background so that the one on the screen runs smoothly. Otherwise **full memory** **slows** everything **down**: the phone moves data back and forth and apps stutter.

That is why good apps save by themselves and regularly, every few seconds. When you build your own, it is the first rule: don’t rely on the app running all the time.

A phone is a computer like any other. The **processor** calculates, working memory holds the work in progress, storage remembers, and **peripherals** (the display, camera, microphone, speaker) are the ways in and out. All of that is **hardware**. Apps and the system are **software**. The system also guards which peripherals an app may use: it won’t get the camera until you allow it.

So when **choosing a phone**, you know what to ask about. More working memory means more apps at once without closing them, more storage means more photos, and a faster processor means smoother games.

**Try it!** Look in your phone’s settings to see how much working memory and how much storage it has, and how much storage is free. Which number is bigger, and why do you think that is?
