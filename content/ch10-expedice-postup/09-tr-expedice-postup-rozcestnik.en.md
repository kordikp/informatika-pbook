Real tools, communities, competitions and projects where you can keep going. This selection is from October 2026: rules, prices and dates change, so always check them on the website. Before you sign up anywhere, read the page [Safe in communities](#bezpecne-v-komunitach-en).

### 🛠 Tools

#### [Blockly Games](https://blockly.games/about)

A set of browser games from Google: Puzzle, Maze, Bird, Turtle (turtle graphics), Movie, Music and Pond. Maze teaches conditions and the “keep to one wall” rule, Turtle teaches nested loops. It is exactly the bridge between the Algorithm core and written code, because the last levels show JavaScript.

*free · no account; made for beginners, suitable from age 10 · the interface has a choice of languages; Czech could not be verified, expect simple English*

**How to start:**

1. Open blockly.games and choose Maze.
2. Go through levels 1 to 9 with as few blocks as possible.
3. In level 10, write one program that gets through every maze without islands.

#### [Lightbot: Code Hour](https://www.lightbot.com/resources.html)

A logic game: a robot lights up tiles with a limited number of commands, later with procedures and loops. The command limit makes you look for the repeating part and build subprograms, which is decomposition in practice.

*Code Hour (20 levels) free with no in-app purchases; the full version with 50 levels is paid · no account, from age 9 · mostly picture-based controls, menu in English*

**How to start:**

1. Install Lightbot: Code Hour (iOS, Android) or play in the browser.
2. Try writing each level a second time, shorter.
3. For levels with a procedure, first draw which piece of the path repeats.

#### [VisuAlgo](https://visualgo.net/)

Animations of algorithms and data structures (sorting, heaps, graphs, trees) from the National University of Singapore; 24 modules, each with a quiz. On your own numbers you can see how many comparisons bubble sort makes and how many merge sort makes. Efficiency from the Test and fix core stops being abstract.

*free · no account, from age 12 (the texts are technical) · English (also Chinese and Indonesian)*

**How to start:**

1. Open visualgo.net and choose Sorting.
2. Enter your own 8 numbers and run Bubble Sort, then Merge Sort.
3. Write down the number of comparisons and compare.

#### [Project Euler](https://projecteuler.net/about)

A collection of maths-and-programming problems where a clever method beats brute force. The problems are built so that a naive program runs for hours and a clever one for a second. The best efficiency training for gifted number-crunchers.

*free · you can read the problems without an account; no age found in the terms, younger users should set up an account with a parent · English; you can solve in any language, even on paper*

**How to start:**

1. Read problem 1 (multiples of 3 and 5).
2. Solve it by hand first for numbers below 10, then with a program.
3. Try to solve it without a loop, using only a formula.

#### [CodinGame (Clash of Code)](https://www.codingame.com/)

Short programming duels: fastest solution, shortest code, or a task you work out from the tests. The “shortest code” mode is the same game as the star for a shorter program in Turtle, only in a real language.

*free · the age limit could not be verified; assume 13+, and younger players only with a parent or in a private club contest · English and French, code in many languages*

**How to start:**

1. With a parent or the club leader, set up a private contest.
2. Start with the Fastest mode in Python.
3. After the duel, read the others’ shortest solutions and find the trick.

#### [CS Unplugged](https://www.csunplugged.org/en/)

Free computing activities without a computer: sorting networks, searching, binary numbers and more. A sorting network on the floor shows a parallel algorithm with your body. Good for a club or a class.

*free · activities for ages 5 to 14, no account · English, German, Spanish, French, Māori, Chinese*

**How to start:**

1. Open the Sorting networks topic.
2. Draw a network for 6 people in chalk.
3. Try designing one for 4 people and test it with all inputs.

### 👥 Communities

#### [Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/2025/about)

*yearly online challenge (1 to 12 December 2025), past years stay open for solving · free (optional AoC++ support) · English; you can solve in any language, even on paper*

**Age:** log in with GitHub or Reddit, so 13+; younger players with a parent’s account or through the club leader

**How to join:**

1. With a parent or the club leader, choose one account for the whole crew.
2. Solve one puzzle a day together, first on the small example from the instructions.
3. Share solutions only among yourselves, not publicly on the day of release.

> **Stay safe:** There has been no global leaderboard since 2025, so there is no race for speed. Don’t put your real name or school in your nickname. In the Reddit discussions, only read, without an account.

#### [CodinGame Clash of Code](https://codingame.com/multiplayer/clashofcode)

*short online programming duels · free · English*

**Age:** age not verified in the terms; assume 13+, younger players only in a private contest with a parent

**How to join:**

1. Play in a private contest with friends from the club, not with strangers.
2. Don’t send anyone your e-mail, phone number or links to your social media.
3. If someone in the chat pushes you to download a file or to pay, leave and tell an adult.

> **Stay safe:** The chat is open to adults. For children, a private contest without public chat is safer.

#### [Project Euler](https://projecteuler.net/about)

*a community of problem solvers (over 150,000 active members in 220 countries) · free · English*

**Age:** problems can be read without an account; younger users should set up an account with a parent

**How to join:**

1. Set up the account with a parent and without your real name.
2. Following the site’s rules, don’t share solutions to problems beyond the first hundred.
3. When you get stuck, write a smaller version of the problem and solve it by hand, instead of looking for a ready-made solution.

> **Stay safe:** It asks for no personal details apart from a nickname and a password. No chat.

#### [Sorting network on the school playground (CS Unplugged)](https://www.csunplugged.org/en/topics/sorting-networks/unit-plan/)

*an event for a club or a class without a computer, team races · free, chalk is all you need · instructions in English, the activity itself needs no language*

**Age:** ages 5 to 14

**How to join:**

1. Agree with a teacher or the club leader where and when you will draw the network.
2. Teams of six, only one team on the network at a time.
3. Only publish photos of others with their consent.

> **Stay safe:** It takes place at school under adult supervision, no accounts.

### 🏆 Competitions and events

#### [EJOI – European Junior Olympiad in Informatics](https://www.ejoi2026.mif.vu.lt/about/)

*olympiad · free when representing your country · tasks in national languages, C++ and others*

The top goal for 13–15-year-old algorithm fans from Hungary as well as from Czechia and Slovakia.

**When:** every summer, in 2026 in Lithuania, check the next one on the website **Age:** junior category (younger than IOI), check the exact limit on the website

**How to join:**

1. nomination by national selection, in Hungary through home competitions and the olympiad

#### [USACO – USA Computing Olympiad](https://usaco.org/)

*online competition · free (training); the website does not mention a competition fee, check · English; C++, Java, Python*

Four divisions (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum) give a clear ladder to climb and a big collection of problems with solutions.

**When:** The 2026/27 schedule has not been published yet (as of October 2026). There are usually four online rounds from December to March, check on the website **Age:** no age limit, students of all levels

**How to join:**

1. an account on usaco.org
2. you start in the Bronze division

#### [picoCTF (Carnegie Mellon)](https://picoctf.org/)

*cybersecurity competition and practice website · free · English*

Ethical hacking in a safe environment. It teaches Linux, cryptography and web security as a game.

**When:** The competition is usually in spring, the picoGym practice website is open all year. Check the 2027 date on the website **Age:** from 13; for ages 13–17 a parent’s e-mail, or a teacher sets up the account

**How to join:**

1. register on picoctf.org with a parent’s e-mail
2. or a class account from a teacher

#### [Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/)

*regular online rounds · free · English/Russian; C++, Python and others*

The rating keeps you motivated and the regular rounds give competition practice for olympiad solvers.

**When:** rounds several times a week **Age:** age limit in the terms not verified

**How to join:**

1. an account on codeforces.com (check the age)
2. start with Div. 3 and Div. 4 rounds

### 🧪 Projects for inspiration

- **[Human sorting network with 50 inputs (Vienna)](https://vcla.at/?p=7584)**: Austrian students built a record-sized sorting network following CS Unplugged and raced on smaller networks for 6 people. *You will learn:* How parallel comparing makes sorting shorter and how a network is checked with all possible inputs.
- **[USACO Guide (source code)](https://github.com/cpinitiative/usaco-guide)**: An open project by students who wrote up a path through competitive programming from the Bronze level to Platinum. *You will learn:* How a big topic can be broken into modules with dependencies: decomposition of a whole textbook.
- **[Advent of Code 2025](https://adventofcode.com/2025/about)**: Twelve December puzzles, each with two parts; the second part changes the task so that the first solution usually isn’t enough. *You will learn:* What a well-built algorithmic problem looks like: a story, a small example, a big input and a trap for slow solutions.
- **[Lightbot and its lesson plans](https://www.lightbot.com/resources.html)**: A logic game whose levels bring in procedures and loops step by step. *You will learn:* How to build a series of levels so that each one adds just one new idea. Useful for your own puzzle collection.

### 📚 Where to keep learning

- **[USACO Guide: General (introduction for beginners)](https://usaco.guide/general)**: A free, well-ordered path through competitive programming: topics, problem sets with solutions, from the Bronze level. *(English)*
- **[CS Unplugged: Sorting networks, lesson plan](https://www.csunplugged.org/en/topics/sorting-networks/unit-plan/)**: Instructions for a sorting network for different age groups, including variations. *(English)*
- **[CS Unplugged Classic: Sorting Networks](https://classic.csunplugged.org/activities/sorting-networks/)**: An older page for the activity, with a Scratch version and advice to test your own network with all input patterns. *(English)*
- **[VisuAlgo: sorting](https://visualgo.net/)**: Step-by-step animations of sorting algorithms with quizzes, good for analysing efficiency. *(English)*
- **[Lightbot: materials for teachers](https://www.lightbot.com/resources.html)**: Lesson plans for the Lightbot levels, inspiration for building your own series of puzzles. *(English)*
